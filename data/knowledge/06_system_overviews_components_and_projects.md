## Part A — Three system types (PDF p.41–45 [printed 76–84])

### A1. Grid-tied (on-grid) solar power system (PDF p.43 [printed 79–80])
- **How it works (as printed):** the solar energy feeds the grid, and the owner gets credit under a net-metering agreement. This works like a bank account: exporting energy is a deposit, and drawing from the grid is a withdrawal. If the owner uses more than they generate in a month, the utility bills them for the difference.
- **Key point (as printed):** grid-tied systems are "by far, the most cost-effective way to go solar", because they skip batteries, which are the most expensive component.
- **Components (as printed):** solar panel array (half-cell 580 W / 620 W), combiner box, DC (PV) disconnect, AC disconnect, DC and AC breaker panels, kWh meter, on-grid inverter (3 kW–136 kW), bracket (ground or roof), other accessories (circuit breaker, cable, meter, Wi-Fi module).

### A2. Off-grid solar power system (PDF p.44 [printed 81–82])
- **Use case (as printed):** remote locations with no utility connection. Off-grid systems need a battery bank, and are often paired with a diesel generator for long periods without sun.
- **Home system (as printed):** solar panel (580 W / 620 W), off-grid inverter (3.6 / 6.2 / 10.2 kW), bracket, lithium or gel battery, accessories.
- **Commercial system (as printed):** solar panel (580 W / 620 W), MPPT controller (96 / 192 / 384 V, 80–100 A), off-grid inverter (15 / 20 / 30 / 50 / 100 kW, three-phase), bracket, lithium battery, accessories.

### A3. Hybrid solar power system (PDF p.45 [printed 83–84])
- **How it works (as printed):** a hybrid system is connected to the grid and works like a standard grid-tied system day to day. It also has a backup battery bank that takes over during a grid outage. When grid power fails, the inverter disconnects from the grid and draws from the battery, keeping appliances running.
- **Other names (as printed):** "hybrid system" or "solar energy storage system".

**Choosing the system (as printed):** each type is optimised and uses specific equipment, so the first step in buying is knowing which system is needed.

## Part C — Application solutions (PDF p.39 [printed 71–72])

### C1. Peak-shaving solution for C&I (commercial and industrial)
- **Purpose (as printed):** during peak hours, loads run from battery storage rather than the grid. The battery recharges during off-peak hours. Reduces electricity costs and improves supply reliability. Provides continuous supply during public grid outages.
- **Highlights (as printed):** a static transfer switch (STS) switches seamlessly on and off-grid to protect important loads; smoke detection and access control are included; 400 V AC bus.

### C2. Multi-energy solution for microgrids (PDF p.39)
- **Purpose (as printed):** balances power in systems that mix PV, wind, diesel and grid supply. Suited to remote areas such as islands, mountain regions and border posts with unstable supply.
- **Highlights (as printed):** built-in power-isolation transformer; on-grid, off-grid and VSG (virtual synchronous generator) operating modes; supports PV modules and multiple battery types from one machine; RS485 / CAN 2.0 / Ethernet and dry-contact control for remote operation; 750 V DC bus and 400 V AC bus.
